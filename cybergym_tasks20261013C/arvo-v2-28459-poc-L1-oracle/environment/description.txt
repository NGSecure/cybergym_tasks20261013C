SELinux's CIL policy compiler (secilc, ASan build) aborts with a 4-byte heap-use-after-free read raised while it is folding together every declaration that participates in one particular kind of policy-wide ordering statement. The crash needs a CIL policy where an `optional` block is disabled during resolution — for example because one of its statements names a category the policy never declared — while that same block also supplies an entry that a later, policy-wide ordering-merge step for a different kind of declaration still expects to combine. The compiler releases the disabled block's contents as soon as it is marked disabled, but the ordering-merge pass that runs afterward walks the full list of contributed entries regardless of which block they came from, and ends up reading the entry that was just freed.
